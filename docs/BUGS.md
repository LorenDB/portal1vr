# Bug log

Severity: 1 = crash, hard lock, or nausea; 2 = feature broken; 3 = wrong with a workaround; 4 = polish or performance.
Bugs stay open until a headset playtest verifies the fix.

Earlier headset findings are recorded in the README and docs/avatar-verification.

| ID | Sev | Found | Bug | Cause | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BUG-001 | 2 | 2026-10-06 full Portal playthrough | escape_02 ending: the player stays on the floor instead of floating up as GLaDOS is pulled into the ceiling. | The map lifts the player with a zero-gravity trigger plus a `point_clientcommand` sending `+jump`/`-jump`. `Hooks::dCreateMove` cleared `IN_JUMP` whenever the VR Jump action was not held, so the map's jump never happened, and a 21 u/s `trigger_push` alone cannot unstick a grounded player. | Fixed (jump now only adds). **Verified 2026-10-07** in the headset: the player floats up with GLaDOS. |
| BUG-002 | 2 | 2026-10-06 full Portal playthrough | After the final cutscene the screen stays black and "Still Alive" never plays. | The credits are a 2D HUD element (`CHudPortalCredits`) whose Paint starts the song, scrolls the lyrics, and ends the roll. The eyes are drawn without the HUD, and with `RenderWindow=0` no pass painted it at all. (With the window on, they would have played on the monitor only.) | `credits.h` reads the client's rolling-credits flags; while they are set, the desktop pass is drawn and shown on the flat screen in the headset. Playtest 2026-10-07: detection fired (`End credits rolling`), the song played and the credits showed on the desktop monitor, but nothing showed in the headset. Suspected: the flat screen is blended by the backbuffer's alpha, which the world pass fills with depth. The credits screen now ignores texture alpha, and flat-screen show/hide is logged with its reason and OpenVR results. **Verified 2026-10-07** in the headset (quick check: `ent_fire relay_blackout trigger` in escape_02); the credits are visible on the flat screen. |
| BUG-003 | 2 | 2026-10-06 full Portal playthrough | Walking in the room felt janky; the view sometimes seemed to lag the walk. | Three roomscale-follow faults: (a) the body's movement was taken out of the head offset a frame late, so the camera surged ahead by up to 1.3 units as the body started and stopped; (b) only movement along the current follow direction was handed over, so walking a curve or turning a corner let the body's sideways momentum drift the camera off the head by up to 20 units; (c) a head leaning through a wall portal near its edge steered the body into the wall beside the opening (pinned for 20 s at 21:45 in that log). | Fixed in `roomscale.h` (render-time credit, follow momentum model, steering into the opening); frame-pacing log added to rule out reprojection. **Verified 2026-10-07**: walking reported fine; the test-chamber log shows regular follow/idle cycles and no blocked follows. |
| BUG-004 | 2 | 2026-10-07, from SteamVR's `vrcompositor.txt` | The game misses the headset's frame rate, and SteamVR on Linux does not reproject, so missed frames repeat; positional movement judders and lags. | Full playthrough (2026-10-06): application CPU 10.8 ms / GPU 11.4 ms average against 11.1 ms at 90 Hz, 112,000 of 273,000 frames repeated, 473 timeouts. escape_02 (2026-10-07): CPU 21.5 ms / GPU 22.5 ms; a test chamber the same evening: CPU 18.3 ms / GPU 18.7 ms, 63% of frames repeated. On an RX 9070 XT, two 2624x2740 eyes of Portal should take a few milliseconds, so render resolution is unlikely to be the limit. More likely suspects: Source draws every eye and portal view on one thread (`mat_queue_mode 0`), and the DXVK present path calls `WaitDeviceIdle()` every frame, so the CPU and GPU never work in parallel. | Open; not yet investigated. The mod logs `Frame pacing` every 10 s when frames are repeated. |
